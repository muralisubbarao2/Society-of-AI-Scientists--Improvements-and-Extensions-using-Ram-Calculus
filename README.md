Abstract
This report consolidates the 106 freshly reconstructed v2 analyses of selected Society of AI Scientists
studies. Their most significant Ram Calculus applications occur at specific intermediate steps:
finite measurement and nuisance calibration, weak and factorial moments, local nonlinear jets,
covariance-aware filtering, and the transfer of approximation errors to normalized outputs or decisions.
The strongest established results are conditional identities and bounds, model-relative estimator
improvements, and counterexamples to overextended interpretations. Examples include a fourthorder
ensemble-loss approximation with smaller synthetic error, a phase-balanced mean with lower
synthetic MSE, finite-history forecasting thresholds, explicit slope-estimation delay and variance, and
a distinction between MAP sampling noise and posterior curvature in difficulty calibration. The series
contains 106 successfully executed companion programs and 938 pages of source reports; these checks
are not 106 replications of the original experiments. Benefits include transparency, identifiable targets,
and testable error budgets. Costs include additional calibration, noisy extrapolation, restrictive
local assumptions, and frequently inconclusive certificates. The summary distinguishes conventional
mathematical ingredients from their Ram organization, provides a thematic synthesis and a sourcelinked
entry for every report, and identifies the original-data experiments still needed to demonstrate
end-to-end advantages.
